Selling a House During Divorce in California: What Order Should Things Happen

The Short Answer Depends on Where You Two Stand

Most of the time, it is workable. I want to say that upfront. Selling a house during divorce in California really does depend on where things stand between the two of you.

Some couples can sit down and agree on a plan. Others are barely speaking. The sequencing question, sell first or settle everything else first, doesn't have one universal answer. What matters most is whether the two of you can make a decision together.

One thing to be clear about: I'm a real estate agent, not an attorney. Nothing in this post is legal advice. If your situation is complicated, you need a family law attorney in your corner. What I can do is walk you through the practical side of how these sales tend to go.

If You Can Agree, You Save More Than You Think

With divorce home sale timing, when both spouses can agree, you have real flexibility. You can list the house anytime before, during, or after the divorce proceedings. You're not locked into any particular window.

What I've seen over the years is that every month of disagreement costs both people money. Attorney fees add up. Carrying costs on the property add up. The emotional toll doesn't get lighter while the calendar moves.

If two people can decide together, you can save a lot of money and heartache and headaches. I mean that plainly. If there's any path toward a mutual decision on the house, it's worth pursuing.

When One Spouse Won't Agree: What California Law Does

Sometimes agreement isn't possible. One spouse wants to sell, the other doesn't. In that case, a judge can order the sale as part of the property division.

California is a community property state. Property division defaults to a 50/50 split, even if one spouse contributed more premarital savings toward the down payment. That surprises a lot of people.

The 50/50 question and everything around it gets decided between the attorneys. Selling a house during divorce in California gets considerably more complicated when attorneys have to sort out the underlying ownership dispute before the property can even be listed. It's not impossible. It just takes longer and costs more.

My Role vs. the Attorneys' Role and Why It Matters

Attorneys handle the legal structure of the divorce. The ownership questions, asset division, court filings, that's their lane. My job is the property itself.

What I actually do in a divorce sale is keep the transaction moving and protect both parties' financial interest in the outcome. That means pricing it right, managing showings, handling negotiations, and making sure neither party leaves money on the table because the process got emotional.

I've handled a lot of divorce home sales over the years. Both parties need someone neutral who knows what they're doing. I'm not on anyone's side except the clean completion of the sale.

Sell Before the Divorce Finalizes If You Can

This is my strongest opinion on the subject. Selling before the divorce finalizes is much cleaner for property division. Fewer loose ends, less back-and-forth after the fact. When the house is sold and proceeds are divided while the divorce is still processing, you're not chasing anything down later.

There's also a tax angle worth knowing, and I'd encourage you to talk to a CPA about your specific situation. This is general context, not tax advice.

Married couples filing jointly can exclude up to $500,000 in capital gains on the sale of a primary residence. Once you're divorced and filing separately, that number drops to $250,000 per person. If your home has appreciated meaningfully, the difference between selling as a married couple versus selling after the divorce is final could matter a great deal.

The capital gains exclusion is one of those things people often find out about too late. Selling before the divorce closes keeps that $500,000 exclusion available. Run the numbers with a tax professional before you decide on timing.

For anyone selling a house during divorce in California who's on the fence about when to list, that tax consideration alone is worth a conversation with your CPA before you make the call.
