Orange County Real Estate Market History Through 40 Years of Cycles

What Four Decades Actually Teaches You

I graduated college and got into real estate right around 1985. South Orange County was a different place then. The numbers were different, the inventory was different, and the buyers were different.

What hasn't changed: the things that actually determine whether a transaction goes well have nothing to do with square footage or list price.

After 40 years in the real estate business, I've learned a lot about homes. I've learned much more about people. That's what Orange County real estate market history actually looks like from the inside. Not a price chart. A long series of human decisions made under pressure, with money on the line and real life on the other side of the closing.

Longevity in one market matters. I'm not talking about 40 years in "real estate" across five different states or a dozen different product types. I'm talking about one stretch of coast, one set of neighborhoods, one market that I've watched breathe in and breathe out over and over again. That kind of specific, cumulative knowledge is different from general industry experience. It tells you things the data doesn't.

Every Cycle Looks Different. The Fear Is Always the Same.

Every South OC housing market cycle I've worked through has had a different trigger. The savings and loan collapse in the late '80s. The early '90s recession. The dot-com correction. 2008. The rate shock after the pandemic boom. Each one had its own fingerprints.

The emotional pattern, though? Almost identical every time.

Buying a home and selling a home is deeply emotional. Clients routinely battle the fear of making the wrong choice, losing money, or facing the unknown. I've seen it in up markets and down markets. I've seen it in cash buyers and first-timers. The dollar amount changes. The fear doesn't.

That's the job. Guide people through those fears. Make sure all their questions get answered. Make sure they know the road ahead before they have to walk it.

I'm not sitting here telling clients what they want to hear. I'm telling them what I've actually seen. A client who understands what's real can make a decision. A client who's been told everything is fine when it isn't? That's where things go sideways.

Understanding how interest rates have shaped this market is one piece of that picture. Rates don't just affect monthly payments. They shift who's in the market, how long homes sit, and what sellers are willing to accept. Every cycle has a rate story running underneath it.

The Math Over Time and What the Numbers Actually Show

Properties in South OC have gone up substantially over the last 40 years. I'm not saying that to pitch you. I'm saying it because I watched it happen, transaction by transaction, neighborhood by neighborhood.

Real estate has been a great investment for most people over the long term. Not every person. Not every purchase. But most people who bought down here and held on came out ahead. Usually well ahead.

That's what long-term real estate investment in Orange County has looked like across every decade I've worked in it. Slow years, fast years, flat years. The long arc goes one direction.

Short-term gains are possible, but the conditions have to be right. You need to be buying substantially below market, and the cost of any improvements has to come out on the right side of the math. If both of those things line up, you can do well in a shorter window. Most of the time, real estate is a long-term hold.

There's more to it than appreciation. When you own, you're not getting a rent increase notice in January. You're not getting told the landlord is selling the place and you've got 60 days. You live in your home. You enjoy it. That stability has real value that doesn't show up in a spreadsheet.

Don't Judge the Buyer in Front of You

This is probably the most useful thing I can pass on after 40 years. It has nothing to do with market cycles or interest rates.

Never judge a client by their appearance.

Some of my most successful and highly qualified buyers have not looked the part. I'm talking about the Orange County stereotype, if you know what that means. You can picture what a buyer shopping for a $2 million home in South OC is expected to look like.

Some of my most qualified clients didn't fit that picture at all. They ended up buying really nice homes and they were very successful. I would have lost those relationships before they started if I'd made assumptions. Don't judge a book by its cover.

When you're thinking about buying a home in South Orange County, you want an agent who takes you seriously from the first conversation, regardless of what you're wearing or how you come across on day one. That's how I approach it.

What This Cycle Reminds Me Of, and What's Actually Different

I've been through a lot of cycles. When people ask me where we are right now in Orange County real estate market history, my honest answer is: I've seen slow before.

The current market has its own specific pressures. Inventory is constrained in ways that are partly a function of rates, partly a function of people who locked in low payments and aren't moving unless they have to. That's a real dynamic and it's worth understanding clearly.

What's consistent with every cycle I've worked through is the emotional pattern I already described. The hesitation. The waiting for perfect conditions that don't come. Then, eventually, life makes the decision and people move.

What's different is the specific combination of rate environment and low supply. That combination is its own thing. I'm not going to tell you this is exactly like 1991 or exactly like 2010, because it isn't. But I've seen enough cycles to know what the noise is and what actually matters.

I've been through enough market cycles to give you a straight answer about where things stand. Not a pitch. Not a forecast dressed up to make you feel good. A real conversation about what this market means for your situation specifically.
